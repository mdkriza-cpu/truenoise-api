The flight path comparison shows that altitude restrictions below 1,200 ft produce no meaningful relief for Severn communities. This tool explores the BRGHT waypoint proposal in detail: drag the slider to vary the turn altitude from the measured current turn (~2,300 ft) to 5,000 ft and see the effect on Ashbrook, Aurora Hills, and Bretton Woods (south of Severn Run), alongside the fuel cost. Anchored to TrueNoise field measurements, the model shows every receptor already well below the N65 threshold at 3,000 ft — because the BRGHT geometry holds aircraft high and moves the concentrated departure burden onto the uninhabited Severn Run parkland. Higher turn altitudes lower the modeled levels further at a small, quantifiable fuel cost; the tool lets you weigh that tradeoff. Noise values are shown as ranges bracketing two propagation assumptions.
